## Finding 1 (blocker) — the grade short-circuit read as stopping on a clean pass

§5.7 said evaluation "returns at the lowest level that yields a definitive
verdict." Policy checks are deterministic by construction, so a clean Policy
*pass* is definitive. Read literally, that rule stops evaluation after any clean
Policy pass — which would make a component selected at Context or Evidence never
run its understanding pass or evidence work whenever Policy passes. That is
exactly the no-op `AT-LF-8` (Context ⊇ Policy) and `AT-LF-9` (Evidence ⊇
Context) exist to forbid. The passage only ever illustrated the rule with a
*fail* example, so the passing case was genuinely ambiguous both ways.

The mechanism was always meant to short-circuit on **failure**, not on success.
A change that fails Policy governance cannot earn a higher assurance, so nothing
higher needs to run — that is the real optimisation. A clean lower-grade result
carries no such implication: the user selected a higher grade precisely to buy
the work above Policy, and that work must run. The correction states this
directly:

- A **failure** at a lower cumulative grade may short-circuit.
- A **clean** lower-grade result continues through the user's selected grade.
- Context executes every Policy check plus its understanding pass, even when
  Policy passed cleanly.
- Evidence executes every Context check plus its evidence work and the §11.3 CI
  gate.
- The reported assurance is the **highest grade actually completed** and can
  never exceed it — the over-claim guard, unchanged.
- Policy stays the ceiling only when Policy itself is the selected grade.

The three failures the rule now names are symmetrical: over-claim (report a
Policy result as Evidence), over-run (silently run above the selected grade), and
the newly-explicit under-run (stop short of the selected grade on a clean
lower-grade pass). Corrected in §5.7 and `AD-32`; the `AT-LF-7`/`AT-LF-8`/
`AT-LF-9` §13 evidence rows now assert the no-op-on-clean-pass case fails. No new
grade axis, no new mechanism, no new acceptance case — the same §5.3/AD-4
cumulative machinery, described without the ambiguity.

## Finding 2 (major) — lower-grade lint-format in a pre-existing Rig CI job

§8.9's generic runner said `.rig/bin/check.js --scope repo` "runs all enabled
repo-applicable controls and selected executable services," while §11.3 tied
lint-format's CI enforcement specifically to the Evidence grade. The two never
reconciled: once a Rig CI job exists for *any* reason (a baseline control, another
service), does a Policy-grade lint-format leaf run in it (§8.9) or stay out
(§11.3)? Gate 1's `AT-LF-13` is silent below Evidence, so this is a Gate-2
technical-design decision, not a Gate-1 question.

Resolution: make repository-CI applicability **grade-aware**, and say so
identically in the generic runner and the lint-format contract. A selected
executable service participates in the repo CI job only when it is CI-applicable
at its *active grade* — a property of the grade, not of whether a Rig CI job
happens to exist. For lint-format that is Evidence only; a Policy- or
Context-grade leaf is listed not-CI-applicable and does not run, even when a Rig
CI job was created for another control or service. Everything else in the CI path
— explicit provider selection, exact plan approval, additive merge,
preservation of unknown/malformed pipelines — is unchanged. Corrected in §8.9
(the authoritative statement), §11.2 (`absent` runner wording), §11.3 (the
reconciling sentence back to §8.9), and the `AT-LF-13` §13 row.

recorded here and left for the claim-string test to honour, not a spec edit.
