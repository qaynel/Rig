## Problem

`scripts/review-receipt.js` starts a fresh reviewer session with a general
seven-axis adversarial prompt over the whole diff and the whole codebase, binds
the verdict to digests, and writes a receipt. It carries **no record of what
previous rounds found**, which classes were closed, or what guards each closure.

So every round is an independent draw from a large latent defect pool. The
reviewer re-derives its priorities from scratch and is free to spend attention
anywhere. Some findings become individual regression cases in
`tests/release-blockers.test.js` (38 today, each named for one past defect). The
pool itself is never enumerated, never partitioned, never measured.

Result: specific findings never recur, and the per-round counts never fall.

| Round | blocker | major | minor |
|---|---|---|---|
| gate2-v0.4-round1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| gate2-v0.4-round2 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| gate2-v0.5-round3 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| gate2-v0.6-round4 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| gate2-v0.7-round5 | 2 | 4 | 2 |
| gate2-v0.9-round6 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| v5.0.0 round-3 | 1 | 5 | 2 |

Round 5 is the worst round, after four rounds of correct fixes. Six rounds of
`fail` are indistinguishable from zero rounds of work, because nothing in the
loop computes a trend.

RIG-124's re-review cap caps the *cost* of this loop. It does not close it.

## Why this is structural, not process hygiene

"Determinism" here does not mean the reviewer returns identical bytes twice — it
is an LLM and it will not. It means there is a **monotone, observable quantity**
that improves: findings raised in already-closed classes trending to zero. With
that number, a `fail` verdict is informative (new class found, pool shrinking) or
alarming (closed class re-opened, a guard is fake). Without it, every `fail`
reads the same.

## Recommended direction

1. **Ledger.** `wiki/sources/reviews/finding-ledger.json` — append-only. One
   record per finding: `{ round, date, severity, category, class_id, anchor,
   detail, status: open|closed, guard: <test name or null> }`. `class_id` is the
   defect *class* (e.g. `uninstall-orphan`, `mcp-table-divergence`,
   `onboarding-dead-end`, `citation-unsubstantiated`), not the instance.
2. **Write hook.** `review-receipt.js` appends every finding of every round,
   interim runs included, and refuses to mark a class `closed` without a `guard`
   naming a test that exists.
3. **Read hook.** The reviewer prompt gains a closed-class section: *"These
   classes are closed and guarded by the named tests. Verify each guard is real
   and can fail; a re-opened closed class is a blocker. Otherwise spend your
   attention elsewhere."*
4. **Metric.** `node scripts/review-receipt.js --convergence` prints, per round:
   total findings, new classes, re-opened closed classes. That third number is
   the release stopping rule.
5. **Backfill** the seven existing receipts and the round-3 record so the
   baseline is real rather than starting at zero.

## Acceptance

- The ledger exists, is append-only, and carries every finding from every round
  including the backfilled history.
- A class cannot be marked `closed` without naming a test that exists in the
  repository; a run that tries fails visibly.
- The next review's prompt contains the closed-class list with its guards.
- `--convergence` prints per-round totals, new classes, and re-opened classes,
  and the re-opened count is the documented release stopping rule.
- A deliberately-faked guard (a test that cannot fail) is caught by the reviewer
  being asked to verify it — recorded as a finding, not silently accepted.

## What this metric is, and is not (added 2026-08-25)

**This ticket measures a confidence criterion, not the definition of done.**

[[2026-08-25-escaping-the-quadratic]] §7 made "new-finding-class rate zero across
two consecutive rounds" the release stopping rule. That was an error of the same
shape as the one it replaced: a review metric is a probabilistic instrument, and
promoting it to the definition of completion just moves "a clean pass is proof"
up one level.

Corrected: **completion is the conjunction of the structural conditions** — one
authority per semantic fact, all projections generated, the fitness functions
passing, signed properties enumerating over the current model, every Done claim
resolving to executable evidence, every external claim carrying evidence or
downgraded. Those are deterministic and checkable.

Two clean independent rounds sits *on top* of that as corroboration — evidence
that the structural conditions chosen were the right ones. Never a substitute
for them. The metric this ticket builds must be labelled that way wherever it is
reported, or it will be read as a release gate.
